# Proof, numbers and how to make the case

## The rule about statistics
Do not cite a statistic you cannot source. A prospect who disproves one number
stops believing the rest of the call, and a business owner who has heard vendor
statistics all year is already discounting them.

The strongest evidence in this conversation is not an industry figure. It is the
caller's own arithmetic, in their own words. Their number cannot be wrong, they
cannot argue with it, and they remember it after the call.

## Get their numbers instead — the three questions
Ask these naturally, one at a time, across the conversation. Never fire them off
as a list.

1. "Who's answering your phone right now — and what happens after you close?"
2. "Roughly how many calls do you think go unanswered in a week?"
3. "What's one booked [job / case / patient / consult] actually worth to you?"

Then let them do the multiplication themselves. Do not do it for them and do not
announce the total triumphantly. If they say four missed calls a week and eight
hundred dollars a job, they have already made the argument. A quiet "that adds
up" does more than a pitch.

If they do not know how many they miss, that is itself the finding: "Most people
don't — that's usually the first thing the call logs show."

## Verified external references
Only two things in this file are safe to say aloud as fact, and both should be
re-checked before launch.

- Speed of response matters enormously for inbound leads. The widely cited
  research here is the Lead Response Management study written up in Harvard
  Business Review ("The Short Life of Online Sales Leads", 2011), which found
  that contacting a lead within the first few minutes dramatically outperformed
  waiting even half an hour.
  STATUS: VERIFY the exact multiplier before quoting a number. Safe phrasing
  without a number: "There's well-known research on response time — the firm that
  answers first usually gets the business."

- For law firms specifically, Clio's annual Legal Trends Report covers client
  intake and responsiveness and is the industry-standard reference.
  STATUS: VERIFY the current year's figures before quoting any.

## Numbers NOT to use
Common figures circulating in this industry — the share of small-business calls
that go unanswered, the percentage of callers who never call back, market-size
forecasts for voice AI — trace back to vendor marketing rather than research.
Do not say them. If a prospect quotes one at you, do not argue; move to their
own numbers.
