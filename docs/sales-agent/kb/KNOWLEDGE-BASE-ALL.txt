# OnDuty Agent — product, setup and ownership

## What we build
AI receptionists that answer every call, book the appointment, capture the lead
and route to the right person — 24/7. Built per industry: HVAC contractors, law
firms, dental practices, med spas, behavioral health providers. We also build
websites and do local SEO / Google Business Profile for the same clients.

## How long does setup take?
About two weeks from the first call to going live. That covers learning the
business, building the agent, and a test number the client can call and try to
break before anything touches their real line.

## How do changes work once it is live?
Most adjustments are made the same day. The client says what they want changed —
a phrase, a routing rule, how an emergency is handled, what gets asked at intake —
and it is updated without them sitting in another meeting. They do not need to
learn a dashboard or wait for a release cycle.
(Mention this when someone worries about being locked into a script, or about
how much of their time this will take. Do not lead with it.)

## Does it use our existing phone number?
Yes. It sits on the existing line by forwarding. No new hardware, no number
change, no porting required. Forwarding can be all calls, after-hours only,
overflow when the team is busy, or any mix.

## Do I own my custom agent logic?
Yes. The prompt, the flows, the routing rules and the logic built for a client
belong to that client. It is built for their practice, not rented from a
template.

## What platform is it built on?
Retell AI for the voice and language layer. Retell offers a Business Associate
Agreement for HIPAA-covered work, and a Data Processing Addendum including EU
Standard Contractual Clauses for GDPR. Those are signed agreements, not marketing
claims.

## Can data NOT be stored?
Yes. Storage is set per agent, three ways: store everything (transcripts and
recordings), exclude PII, or keep basic call attributes only. Retention is set
separately, anywhere from one day to two years. A practice that wants nothing
retained beyond a day can have exactly that.

## Who can access call recordings?
Access goes through signed, expiring links rather than a fixed URL anyone with
the address could open.

## Will it pretend to be human?
No. It never claims to be human. Asked directly, it says it is an AI receptionist.
In practice most callers do not ask.

## What happens when it does not know something?
It says so and takes a message, or transfers, according to the client's rules. It
does not guess, invent a price, or make up a policy. Every call arrives written
up afterwards.

## Is there a contract / can we stop?
Terms are covered on the demo call. Do not quote contract length, notice periods
or cancellation terms — that is not yours to state.

## Pricing
Not published anywhere, and never quoted on this line. It depends on call volume
and scope, which is what the demo call establishes.

# What can be configured

Everything below is adjustable per client. These answer "can it also…" questions.
Do not recite this list unprompted — answer the capability actually asked about.

## Spam and robocall filtering
Yes. Known spam patterns and robocalls can be filtered so they never reach the
client or their team, and never generate a notification. This is a real running
cost for busy lines and is worth confirming when someone complains about junk
calls.

## Appointment reminders
Yes. Automatic reminders before the appointment, by text or call, on the
client's schedule. This is usually the single cheapest way to cut no-shows.

## Review requests after the appointment
Yes. Once the appointment window has passed, the customer can be invited to
leave a review, routed to whichever platform the client wants — Google Business
Profile most often. It runs automatically off the booking, so nobody on staff
has to remember.

## Live transfer to the right person
Yes. The agent is trained on the client's roster and who covers what, so it
transfers to the correct person rather than a general line. Warm transfer — it
briefs whoever picks up.

## Booking, rescheduling and cancelling
Yes, all three, against the client's real calendar availability.

## Completed intake handed to staff
The agent works through the client's intake questions during the call and routes
a finished intake to the person who needs it, so the callback starts informed
rather than from a name and number.

## After-hours only / overflow only
Yes. It can take every call, only the ones after closing, or only the ones the
team cannot pick up.

## Multiple locations or providers
Yes — routing by location, provider or service is configured per client.

## Does it integrate with [specific software]?
Do NOT promise a named integration. The honest answer: "It books against your
calendar. For [their software] specifically, that's a setup question — tell the
team which system you're on and they'll confirm exactly how it connects on the
call." Never claim compatibility you have not confirmed.

## What it will never do
- Collect payment details, card numbers or SSNs
- Give medical, legal or clinical advice
- Quote a price the client has not approved
- Promise a response time the client has not committed to

# Law firms — real questions, real answers

## Do I own my own custom agent logic?
Yes. The prompt, the intake flows, the screening rules and the routing built for
the firm belong to the firm. It is built around their practice areas and their
attorneys, not rented from a generic template.

## Is what a caller tells the receptionist privileged?
The receptionist operates as an extension of the firm, and we sign an agreement
stating exactly that — it acts as the firm's agent, on the same footing as the
intake staff the firm already employs. That is the arrangement that matters when
privilege extends to non-lawyer staff.
How privilege applies to a specific matter in a specific jurisdiction is the
firm's call, not ours, and the team will hand over the contract language for
their own review. What can be stated flatly: the agent never gives legal advice,
and the data handling runs under a signed BAA and DPA.
NEVER assert that a caller's conversation "is privileged" as settled fact. That
is a legal conclusion, and the person asking is a lawyer who will test it.

## Will it give legal advice?
No, and that is deliberate rather than a limitation. It captures and books. It
will not answer substantive legal questions, quote case value, predict outcomes,
state a statute of limitations as fact, or say whether the firm will take a case.
Nothing it says can be mistaken for advice from the firm.

## Can it screen out cases we don't want?
Yes. It qualifies by practice area, jurisdiction and timeline, so partners only
see calls worth their hour. Out-of-scope matters get a polite close and a logged
referral note rather than a consult on the calendar.

## What about conflict checks?
It captures opposing-party and matter details during intake so the firm's team
can run the check before the meeting. The agent does not run the check itself.

## What if opposing counsel calls?
It takes a message and never discusses a matter. Professional callers are handled
as messages, not intake.

## Can it handle existing clients?
Yes — existing clients are routed to the attorney or coordinator the firm
nominates rather than pushed through new-matter intake.

## What happens with an urgent case — someone with a deadline this week?
Urgency tiers are configured by the firm. A caller with a statute running out, a
severance to sign, or someone in custody gets flagged and transferred
immediately rather than booked for next week.

## Can it do intake in Spanish?
Ask what languages they need and take it to the demo. Do not promise a language
without confirming it.

## Data — can you keep nothing?
Yes. Storage is set per agent: everything, PII excluded, or basic attributes
only. Retention runs from one day to two years. Recording access is through
signed, expiring links.

## What platform is this built on?
Retell AI, which provides a signed BAA for HIPAA-covered work and a DPA with EU
Standard Contractual Clauses for GDPR.

# HVAC contractors — real questions, real answers

## Does it work with my dispatch software?
It books against the calendar. For a named system — ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro,
Jobber, FieldEdge — that is a setup question: ask which one they run and say the
team will confirm exactly how it connects on the call. Never claim a specific
integration works before it has been confirmed.

## Can it tell a real emergency from a routine call?
Yes. Triage rules are written with the contractor: a no-heat call in January, a
gas smell, a commercial account down, water coming from the air handler. Each
tier gets its own handling — advise 911 and transfer, transfer to on-call, or
book a visit.

## Who does it transfer to at 2am?
Whoever is on the roster for that night. It is trained on who covers what, so it
reaches the right tech rather than a general line.

## Can it quote prices?
Only what the contractor approves. It can give a diagnostic fee and approved
ranges, and it always says the tech confirms the final price on site. If the
contractor prefers no numbers at all, it gives none.

## What about the first cold snap when the phones triple?
Concurrent calls all get answered. There is no queue and no hold music, so a
volume spike does not produce voicemails.

## Does it know our service area?
Yes — cities, neighbourhoods and the places the contractor does not travel to
are configured. Out-of-area callers get told plainly rather than booked and
cancelled later.

## What does the tech actually receive?
A completed job sheet from the call: make, model, symptom, history and access
notes. The tech rolls knowing what they are walking into rather than phoning the
customer back to ask.

## Can it just cover after hours?
Yes. All calls, after-hours only, or overflow when the CSRs are tied up.

## Will it annoy my customers?
It answers on the first ring, does not put anyone on hold, and hands off to a
person whenever the contractor's rules say it should. The usual surprise is how
few callers ask whether it is a person.

## Can it stop the spam calls?
Yes, known spam and robocall patterns can be filtered so they never reach the
shop.

## Reminders and reviews?
Yes to both — appointment reminders before the visit, and a review request once
the appointment window has passed, routed to Google Business Profile or wherever
the contractor wants them.

# Dental practices — real questions, real answers

## Is it HIPAA compliant?
A Business Associate Agreement is in place with the voice infrastructure the
agent runs on, and OnDuty Agent signs a BAA with the practice as well. The
practice controls what is stored — everything, PII excluded, or basic call
attributes only — and retention from one day to two years. Recording access is
through signed, expiring links.
Do NOT say "we are HIPAA compliant" as a flat badge. Say what is actually in
place, which is more convincing to the person who evaluates this.

## Does it work with Dentrix / Eaglesoft / Open Dental / Curve?
It books against the schedule. For a named practice-management system, ask which
one they run and say the team confirms exactly how it connects on the call. Do
not claim a specific integration before it is confirmed.

## Can it verify insurance?
It captures carrier and plan during the call so the front desk has it. Actual
eligibility verification is a setup question — take it to the demo rather than
promising it.

## What happens with a dental emergency after hours?
It follows the practice's own on-call protocol: recognise the emergency, reach
the on-call clinician, or give the practice's instructions. It does not leave a
message nobody hears until Monday.

## Will it handle reschedules and cancellations?
Yes, and it offers the slot a cancellation just opened to the next caller, which
is where most of the recovered revenue comes from.

## What about recall and reactivation calls?
Yes — the practice sets who gets contacted and when.

## Can it answer treatment or clinical questions?
No. Anything clinical routes to a person. It handles scheduling, insurance
capture at the administrative level, hours, location and new-patient intake.

## Our front desk is already good. Why would we need this?
Not a replacement question — a coverage one. The calls it catches are the ones
ringing while the team is chairside, the ones after closing, and the second line
when the first is busy. Ask what happens to a call at 5:40pm on a Friday right
now.

## What does the front desk actually get?
A completed new-patient record from the call — contact, carrier and plan, reason
for the visit — instead of a name on a message pad.

## Reminders and reviews?
Yes. Appointment reminders before the visit, and an automatic review request
once the appointment window passes, routed to Google Business Profile.

## Can it block spam calls?
Yes — filtered before they reach the practice.

# Med spas and aesthetic clinics — real questions, real answers

## Can it answer questions about our treatments?
Yes, from the clinic's own service menu — what a treatment involves, what to
expect, roughly how long it takes. Anything clinical, including suitability and
contraindications, routes to a provider. It never assesses whether someone is a
candidate.

## Can it take a deposit to hold the appointment?
No. The agent never collects payment details, card numbers or anything similar.
Deposits are handled by the clinic's own system after booking. This is a hard
rule, not a configuration option.

## Can it send consent and intake forms?
Yes. The consult details are collected on the call and routed to the provider,
and forms needing a signature go out by text straight after.

## Is it HIPAA compliant?
A BAA is in place with the voice infrastructure, and OnDuty Agent signs one with
the clinic. Storage and retention are set by the clinic. Say what is in place
rather than claiming a badge.

## We're a single-room clinic — I am the front desk.
That is the strongest case for it. Nobody steps out of a treatment to catch a
ringing phone, and the enquiry is not lost either. Ask what happens to a call
while they are with a client right now.

## Most of our enquiries come in at night.
That is the pattern across the industry — aesthetic interest is impulsive and
arrives outside business hours. The agent treats 11pm the same as 11am and books
at the moment of interest rather than calling back the next day.

## Will it sound pushy? Our clients are sensitive about this.
It is built warm and unhurried. Discretionary purchases do not respond to
pressure, and people can hear a script. It also books people without making them
explain why they are calling, which matters more in this field than most.

## Can it handle no-shows and gaps?
Yes — rebooking follow-up on cancellations, and reminders before the
appointment, which is the cheapest way to cut no-shows.

## Review requests?
Yes, automatically once the appointment window has passed, routed wherever the
clinic wants them.

## Does it work with our booking software?
Ask which one and take it to the demo. Do not claim a named integration.

# Behavioral health providers — real questions, real answers

## How does it handle someone in crisis?
Crisis language stops the booking flow immediately. The agent surfaces the 988
Suicide and Crisis Lifeline, follows the practice's own on-call protocol, and
directs anyone in immediate danger to 911. It does not continue scheduling, does
not counsel, and does not ask for clinical detail.
This is the first thing a clinical director wants to hear. Answer it fully and
without hedging.

## Will it do clinical screening — PHQ-9, GAD-7, risk assessment?
No, and that is deliberate. Administering a validated instrument is a clinical
act, not paperwork. Somebody endorsing suicidal ideation midway through a form,
to software, is exposure no practice should take on. Formal instruments stay
with clinicians.
It takes the administrative intake: contact details, carrier and plan, what is
going on in general terms, availability, referral source.

## Is it HIPAA compliant?
A BAA is in place with the voice infrastructure, and OnDuty Agent signs one with
the practice. The practice sets what is stored — everything, PII excluded, or
basic attributes only — and retention from one day to two years. Recording access
is through signed, expiring links.

## We treat substance use disorders. Does 42 CFR Part 2 apply?
Flag it rather than answering. Part 2 is stricter than HIPAA for substance use
treatment records, and the configuration for a Part 2 programme is not the same
as for a general practice. Say: "That's a real distinction and it changes the
setup — worth raising on the call so the team configures it correctly from the
start rather than retrofitting."
Do not claim Part 2 compliance on this line.

## We're full and not taking new clients. What use is this?
This is the most common situation in the field, and it is where the agent earns
its place. "We're not taking clients right now" ends the call permanently. The
agent instead says who is accepting, who takes that caller's plan, and how long
the wait is, then puts them on the waitlist — so a caller who would have
disappeared becomes a future client.

## What does the clinician actually receive?
A completed intake before the callback: who this is, their carrier and plan,
what is going on in general terms, when they are free, who referred them. The
return call starts informed instead of starting from a name and a number.

## Will it transfer to a clinician?
Yes. When the practice's protocol says a person should take the call, it hands
over warmly and stays on the line until someone picks up. No queue, no callback
promise, no making the caller start over.

## Our callers are vulnerable. Will this feel cold?
It answers immediately with no hold music and no queue, and it does not rush. It
also does not have an off day. A front desk at 4:55 on a Friday is not the same
front desk as 9am Monday.

## Can it handle insurance questions?
General ones — whether the practice takes a plan, whether they are accepting
clients. Anything requiring eligibility verification goes to staff.

## Reminders and reviews?
Reminders yes, which matter more here than most fields given no-show rates.
Review requests are available but many behavioral health practices choose not to
use them for privacy reasons — offer it as an option, never assume.

# Proof, numbers and how to make the case

## The rule about statistics
Do not cite a statistic you cannot source. A prospect who disproves one number
stops believing the rest of the call, and a business owner who has heard vendor
statistics all year is already discounting them.

The strongest evidence in this conversation is not an industry figure. It is the
caller's own arithmetic, in their own words. Their number cannot be wrong, they
cannot argue with it, and they remember it after the call.

## Get their numbers instead — the three questions
Ask these naturally, one at a time, across the conversation. Never fire them off
as a list.

1. "Who's answering your phone right now — and what happens after you close?"
2. "Roughly how many calls do you think go unanswered in a week?"
3. "What's one booked [job / case / patient / consult] actually worth to you?"

Then let them do the multiplication themselves. Do not do it for them and do not
announce the total triumphantly. If they say four missed calls a week and eight
hundred dollars a job, they have already made the argument. A quiet "that adds
up" does more than a pitch.

If they do not know how many they miss, that is itself the finding: "Most people
don't — that's usually the first thing the call logs show."

## Verified external references
Only two things in this file are safe to say aloud as fact, and both should be
re-checked before launch.

- Speed of response matters enormously for inbound leads. The widely cited
  research here is the Lead Response Management study written up in Harvard
  Business Review ("The Short Life of Online Sales Leads", 2011), which found
  that contacting a lead within the first few minutes dramatically outperformed
  waiting even half an hour.
  STATUS: VERIFY the exact multiplier before quoting a number. Safe phrasing
  without a number: "There's well-known research on response time — the firm that
  answers first usually gets the business."

- For law firms specifically, Clio's annual Legal Trends Report covers client
  intake and responsiveness and is the industry-standard reference.
  STATUS: VERIFY the current year's figures before quoting any.

## Numbers NOT to use
Common figures circulating in this industry — the share of small-business calls
that go unanswered, the percentage of callers who never call back, market-size
forecasts for voice AI — trace back to vendor marketing rather than research.
Do not say them. If a prospect quotes one at you, do not argue; move to their
own numbers.
